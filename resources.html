What resources were used?

A wide range of resources were necessary for this project. Through the use of software/hardware/materials/University departments we were able to design, and create a Pterodactyl.

What departments were available?

There were many University Departments that were made available for us to use in order to achieve a result. Examples of which are:

  • 3D scanning hardware, software, and workshop facilities
  • Motion capture hardware, software, and stage facilities
  • Computer Aided Design (CAD) software package
  • 3D modelling software
  • Haptic input hardware and associated 3D sculpting software
  • 3D printing hardware and workshop facilities
  • Laser cutting hardware and workshop facilities
  • Resin production and postproduction, and composite workshop facilities

3D scanning workshop facilities could be used to create a 3D model of the final product where all structural components are assembled (base, body and wings) with the skin attached, however due to a time restriction we didnt have time to take advantage of this facility.
Motion capture facilities could be utilised to capture the flapping of the wings to demonstrate how the joints actually move around instead of using this we used, we were also able to download assets that had the same mobility.
Computer aided design (CAD) and 3D modeeling software were used to design the prototype and the final models so that they were able to be sent through to the 3D Printing department.
3D printing facilities can be used for printing off the models which were important to print our prototypes.
3D sculpting software facilities werent used as it wont be very accessible.
Laster cutter facilities could have been used to create a base to ensure that the pterodactyl has a stable stand, however by 3D printing the base we were able to ensure the size fits in keeping it securely fastened without the use of screws or pins which may splinter the plastic.
Resin facilities weren't suitable for the project so they werent used because of the size of our artifact being 27cm long and 60cm wide so finding a 3D printer that has the size would be difficult and even if one were to be found it would also an unreasonable cost we would have to endure as we would not be certain that the first print would be successful.

Software

There were a range of different softwares that we used as a group.
Blender was used to design and model the Pterodactyl which was then sent to the 3D department so that we could get it printed by professionals which reduced our personal risk. To ensure that the model was printable the technicions at the 3D laboratory used the UltiMaker Cura software to place supports, structures, and decide how much to fill it by.

Microsoft Applications were used for a variety of different reasons.

  • Microsoft Word - This was used to maintain a clear track on what has been completed, create a Risk Assessment and it was used to state our goals.
  • Microsoft Excel - Excel was used to create a Gantt chart so we are able to try to stay on track, and we used it to make a cost spread sheet so that we were able to budget efficiently.
  • Microsoft Paint - Paint was used to collaberate ideas in a simple way so we could demonstrate certain mechanisms.

Hardware

Two pieces of hardware were used to be able to develop our prototype.
One being a PC as it is capable of handling design softwares and it is also able to handle UltiMaker Cura. The other form of hardware used for this project, was the 3D printer which was the main element as without a 3D printer a tangible product would not be viable.

Materials

This section will be discussing the variety of materials that were used and the alternative options so that an understanding of our choice is comprehensible.

    Model Material:
  • Polylactic Acid (PLA) - This material is commonly used in prototypes because of its composition. Being made from renewable resources like sugarcane and cassava roots, allowing it to degrade in a more environmentally friendly way, requiring constant and high temperatures alongside controlled moisture levels typically above 60°C((Biology Insights, 2026; 3DSCAPERS, 2026) . PLA is also known for its dimensional stability and rigidity, this means that it cools quickly and it prints accurately making it ideal for prototypes. The artefact prototypes were printed using PLA fillament because of its high precision so we are able to identify any major flaws in the design. The final artefact was going to be printed with PLA fillament too however due to a lack of slots we weren't able to print the final project using the Ultimaker so we resorted to a different fillament for the final artefact.
  • Polyethylene Terphthalate Glycol (PETG) - This fillament is a widely used in 3D printing because of its durable, impact-resistant, and temperature resistance properties(Science Insights, 2026) . These properties turned out to be ideal for reasons that werent fully considered before the switch.
    Including: Temperature Resistance: Although temperature resistance is a main property it made it ideal in comparison to PLA which starts to soften at 60°C. Considering the intended environment it'll be in it could start to soften as it would be a black fillament causing it to absorb heat and during the summer while its in the case it could start to malform. UV resistence: ASA fillament is more UV resistent however as it is harder to print than PETG it wasnt worth the trade off as ASA is generally more expensive than PETG too. The UV properties are also not ideal as in the Uk the average UV being 3.24 and the maximum being 8.8 in June (Windrush Weather, 2025) which was an irregularity this means that it wasnt a a necessary trade-off and PETG is also better at handling UV than PLA which is another advantage of using PETG instead. Durability: Thirdly, PETG is more durable than PLA so in the possible scenario that we have to consider of the children interacting with the Pterodactyl artefact this ensures that it doesnt break at the hands of the children. Furthermore, the glycol acid also ensures that the plastic doesn't splinter when being printed ensuring it's likelihood of splintering is at a minimum so the risk of injuring either the children, teachers, or technicions is almost minimal.
